## Article draft for editorial review

### Measure PFD: three questions to ask before voting

Pasadena's November 3 ballot asks voters to decide Measure PFD, a proposed tax on improved property to fund specified fire facilities, equipment, and emergency-response services. Before deciding, residents can examine three practical questions: what the tax costs, what funding already exists, and what the proposed ordinance requires.

The proposed annual charge is $0.19 per taxable square foot, subject to exemptions. For a 1,600-square-foot property, that is $304 a year, or $4,256 over 14 years if the taxable area stays the same. Actual liability depends on the property's taxable area and eligibility for exemptions. The proposal includes qualifying low-income owners, certain owners with senior or disability utility-tax exemptions, and qualifying government, religious, or community-service property.

Existing fire funding needs a careful reading. Pasadena's adopted FY 2027 to 2031 capital plan lists $7.675 million appropriated through FY 2026 for its station renovation program. The same project history describes completed improvements at Station 33 and a completed retrofit and tenant improvements at Station 38. Appropriated dollars are authorized funding, not a verified total of invoices paid.

Full station replacement is a different question. The plan's future-projects section lists replacements of Stations 37 and 33 as unfunded, with planning estimates subject to further definition or revision. Completed repairs do not mean a full replacement has already been paid for.

Measure PFD's proposed ordinance includes reconstruction of Stations 33 and 37 within its first ten years. It also restricts eligible spending and requires annual audits and public reporting. These provisions should be evaluated alongside the ordinance's actual funding and service requirements.

A Yes vote supports adopting the proposed tax. A No vote opposes it. Passage requires at least two-thirds of votes cast on Measure PFD. Rejection would leave future capital and service decisions to the City's budget process and other applicable requirements; it does not establish a guaranteed future service level.

Our No on Measure PFD campaign opposes this tax and believes existing resources should be prioritized for fire facilities. Voters can check the proposal in the City's ordinance and use our linked guides to examine the cost, funding history, and voting choices for themselves.

Suggested contextual link: [Measure PFD funding comparison and timeline](https://www.pfdno.com/measure-pfd-vs-measure-i/).

Primary sources for the editor: [ordinance](https://www.cityofpasadena.net/fire/wp-content/uploads/sites/25/MEASURE-PFD-RESOLUTION-PARCEL-TAX-MEASURE.pdf); [municipal facilities capital plan, page 1.2](https://www.cityofpasadena.net/public-works/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/Tab-03-Municipal-Building-and-Facilites.pdf#page=4); [future projects, pages 1.19–1.20](https://www.cityofpasadena.net/public-works/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/Tab-17-Future-Projects-1.pdf#page=6).
